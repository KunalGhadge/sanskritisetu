Overview

The Stone Chariot is one of India's most celebrated architectural wonders, representing the zenith of Vijayanagara granite craftsmanship. Dedicated to Garuda — the celestial mount of Lord Vishnu — it stands as the axial focal point facing the grand Vittala Temple sanctum.

Architecture

Though it appears carved from a single colossal boulder, the chariot is masterfully engineered from interlocking granite slabs. Four monumental wheels (1.2m diameter each) adorned with 16-spoke concentric floral hubs were originally engineered to rotate freely on granite axles. Height: 4.5m · Base: 4.2m × 3.6m. Material: fine-grained Grey and Pink Deccan Granite.

Historical Significance

Commissioned under Emperor Krishnadevaraya (1509–1529 CE) following his victorious Konark campaign. The Vijayanagara Empire's imperial capital fell after the Battle of Talikota (1565 CE), yet the granite chariot remained intact. Inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1986 (Site #356) for its exceptional universal artistic value.
